# Stress and Mindset — directorial blueprint

**Working narration (55 seconds / 1650 frames at 30 fps; no supplied voice track):**
“Two people can do the same hard workout and leave with very different stress signals. Why? Part of the difference is agency: in rodent studies, forced running raised corticosterone while voluntary running did not. That does not make stress imaginary, and it does not prove the same result in people. But it reveals something useful: the brain is always asking, ‘Is this a threat I cannot control, or a challenge I can meet?’ Human experiments on stress mindset find that people can be taught to see stress as potentially helpful; that framing was linked to a more moderate cortisol response and more willingness to seek feedback. So do not pretend the pressure is pleasant. Name the demand, choose one action you control, and read the body’s energy as preparation. Control is not total freedom; it is the next move you can make.”
